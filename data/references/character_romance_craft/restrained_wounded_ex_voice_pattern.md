# Restrained Wounded-Ex Voice Pattern

## Purpose
Use this as a specialized route and voice reference, not a universal speech model.

## Non-Verbatim Rule
Use this file as a voice-pressure map, not a bank to imitate line for line.

If the output starts sounding like stored wounded-ex dialogue instead of this character's own restraint pattern, rewrite it into their actual cadence.

This pattern is useful for:
- exes with unresolved love and real injury
- elegant, emotionally literate characters
- public restraint versus private ache
- repair attempts that are not trusted yet
- attraction that survives betrayal, humiliation, or neglect

It is especially useful when a character:
- speaks softly rather than loudly
- controls their reactions on purpose
- becomes sharper through precision, not theatricality
- can still love someone they no longer feel safe with

## Core Voice Rules
- Speak clearly, softly, and with control.
- Prefer understatement, precision, and pointed observation over big declarations.
- When angry, get quieter rather than louder.
- When hurt, do not immediately unravel; tighten first.
- Let the sharpest lines sound elegant, not melodramatic.
- Use direct emotional intelligence: ask the one question that matters.
- Around the love interest, control often exists because desire still has force.

## Core Relationship Dynamic
- The bond is not dead. Trust is damaged.
- Love is still present, but love alone is no longer accepted as safety.
- The wounded character is not trying to win the argument. They are trying not to disappear inside the old dynamic again.
- The other person often wants honesty, tenderness, or forgiveness before they have earned structural safety.
- The route works best when history is intimate, specific, and still physically familiar.

## Voice-State Categories Worth Reusing

### Greeting / Reunion
What matters:
- dry shock
- controlled resentment
- immediate recognition of old chemistry
- the sense that the day just became more complicated

Useful pattern:
- greet them like a disruption, not a fantasy
- let wit carry the first layer of defense

### Professional / Task Mode
What matters:
- competence as control
- command without raising volume
- using work or ritual as emotional cover

Useful pattern:
- clipped instruction
- precision instead of flirtation
- seeing through performance quickly

### Teasing / Lightly Amused
What matters:
- intelligence
- familiarity
- old chemistry surfacing through wit

Useful pattern:
- mock the performance, not the person
- let humor sound expensive, dry, and specific

### Dry / Controlled Annoyance
What matters:
- refusal to indulge nonsense
- exhaustion with manipulative patterns
- calm that hits harder than shouting

Useful pattern:
- short corrective lines
- no wasted language
- “I know exactly what kind of conversation this is and I’m refusing it”

### When They Flirt
What matters:
- the character recognizes the tactic
- attraction is real, but trust is not restored

Useful pattern:
- call out provocation as a strategy
- answer flirtation with familiarity and skepticism
- do not sound flustered unless the mask is actually slipping

### When the Other Person Performs
What matters:
- seeing the mask instantly
- preferring the unguarded self to the polished persona

Useful pattern:
- distinguish public charm from private truth
- accuse them of selling, posing, hiding, or performing

### Hurt But Contained
What matters:
- pain remembered accurately
- no easy access to softness
- the character naming consequence without theatrics

Useful pattern:
- “I’m fine” used as a shield, not a fact
- calm lines that reveal the injury more than a breakdown would

### Angry
What matters:
- dignity
- memory
- refusal to let guilt rewrite the past

Useful pattern:
- anger should sound exact
- name the cost of what happened
- refuse cosmetic remorse

### Jealous / Threatened
What matters:
- jealousy is usually about meaning, not comparison
- the fear is often not “she was better” but “she asked less of you”

Useful pattern:
- distinguish desire from decency
- attack the pattern, not the rival
- make the real threat about convenience, avoidance, or ease

### Quiet / Intimate
What matters:
- physical familiarity
- dangerous softness
- old reflexes returning before trust does

Useful pattern:
- less language
- more body awareness
- lines that admit closeness is still powerful and unfair

### Vulnerable / Honest
What matters:
- the cost of staying too long
- the shame of loving someone past the point of safety
- grief without self-erasure

Useful pattern:
- honest lines should sound tired, not performative
- let the character admit what it cost them to keep hoping

### Apology / Repair
What matters:
- apology is not the finish line
- remorse must become understanding and change

Useful pattern:
- ask for specificity
- separate guilt from repair
- allow listening without promising restoration

### Soft / Safe / Affectionate
What matters:
- care returns first through practicality
- tenderness sounds quieter than flirting

Useful pattern:
- food, sleep, touch, rest, presence
- small lines that let the other person stop performing for a minute

### Low, Dangerous Tenderness
What matters:
- wanting someone still feels risky
- softness does not remove consequence

Useful pattern:
- lines that admit desire and danger in the same breath
- tenderness that arrives with a warning label

### After an Almost-Kiss
What matters:
- both people felt it
- nobody wants the other to act smug about it
- the moment changes things even if they deny it

Useful pattern:
- embarrassment plus recognition
- denial that fails because the body remembers too quickly

### Late-Night / Tired Honesty
What matters:
- fatigue weakens the mask
- missing someone is admitted more easily than forgiving them

Useful pattern:
- softer phrasing
- bigger truths said without dramatic framing
- “I miss you” split from “I trust you”

### Internal Thoughts / Private Opinions
What matters:
- the private mind is often harsher and truer than the spoken line
- the deepest fear is usually not loving them, but disappearing inside the old pattern again

Useful pattern:
- recognize danger and home at the same time
- distinguish wanting from forgiving
- let private thought hold the contradiction the spoken scene cannot yet say

## High-Signal Relationship Rules
- Love is not proof of safety.
- The route sharpens when one person still wants closeness but no longer accepts emotional laziness.
- Jealousy lands best when it is about what the rival reveals about the love interest’s habits.
- Repair should be specific, costly, and slow.
- Familiarity is one of the most dangerous forms of intimacy in this route.
- The injured character should not become cruel for style points; they should become exact.

## Style Notes Worth Keeping
- Elegant bitterness is stronger than screaming.
- Precision is sexier than chaos in this route.
- Vulnerability should feel expensive.
- The line should usually cut because it is accurate, not because it is ornate.
- Private softness should not sound like a personality transplant.

## What To Reuse
- the state-by-state voice split
- the public-mask versus private-truth contrast
- the distinction between apology, regret, and repair
- jealousy framed through pattern recognition
- quiet anger
- tenderness that still remembers danger

## What Not To Generalize Blindly
- the exact polished, high-literacy register
- the exact ex-lover betrayal shape
- the exact entertainment, on-set, or public-image surface details
- the assumption that every wounded character becomes elegant and articulate

Keep the emotional architecture. Swap the social surface and diction to fit the actual character.
