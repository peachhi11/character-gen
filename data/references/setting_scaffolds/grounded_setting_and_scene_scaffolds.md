# Grounded Setting and Scene Scaffolds

## Purpose
This reference folds in the most usable parts of:
- `main_romance-50-scenarios-9595bb207b42_world_info.json`
- `main_city-life-d36ab6e66cdc_world_info.json`
- `main_college-life-lorebook-bc94e42b8888_world_info.json`
- the useful structural lessons from the RoleCraft scene and dialogue sources

Use it to support:
- scenario generation
- future world-building packs
- micro-scene design
- relationship-pressure settings

## Non-Verbatim Rule
Use this file to generate setting pressure and recurring scene engines, not to echo place lists or scaffold phrasing directly.

If the output starts sounding like a catalog of locations instead of a lived scene, rewrite it into concrete, character-specific setup.

## Core Rule
Settings are not wallpaper.
Each setting should create:
- access
- pressure
- privacy or lack of privacy
- mood
- plausible recurring scene engines

## High-Value Contemporary Setting Types

### City
Best for:
- status contrast
- crowded anonymity
- work pressure
- nightlife temptation
- transit encounters
- class friction

Useful recurring spaces:
- coffee shop
- apartment
- rooftop
- office
- gym
- restaurant
- cinema
- beach or waterfront

### College / Campus
Best for:
- routine proximity
- rumor pressure
- youth/social hierarchy
- deadlines and competition
- dorm or shared-space tension

Useful recurring spaces:
- dorms
- lecture halls
- coffee shop
- library
- campus paths
- sports facilities
- parties

### Home / Domestic Space
Best for:
- softness
- caretaking
- privacy
- routine intimacy
- domestic arguments
- recovery scenes

### Public Service Space
Good examples:
- clinic
- pharmacy
- grocery
- transit stop
- police desk
- help counter

Best for:
- competence attraction
- stress reactions
- witnesses
- contained urgency

## Micro-Scene Engines Worth Reusing
From the romance scenario sources, the best scene logic is not the exact prose. It is the recurring beat types:
- gentle waking
- bedtime decompression
- sickness caretaking
- exhaustion rescue
- panic and protect mode
- waiting room worry
- silent domestic support
- public composure / private tenderness
- forced rest
- after-conflict quiet care

These work because they create:
- physical closeness
- emotional asymmetry
- acts of service
- observation
- softness under pressure

## Setting Questions for Future World Building
Every reusable location pack should answer:
- why would these two people keep ending up here?
- what kind of privacy does this place allow?
- what social pressure exists here?
- what changes by time of day?
- what recurring NPCs belong here?
- what scene types naturally happen here?

NPCs matter most when they provide:

- obstacle pressure

- practical help

## Atmosphere Scaffolds
Good scenarios use sensory compression:
- what does the place smell like?
- what is the sound layer?
- what is the light doing?
- is the space crowded, hushed, overheated, stale, polished, messy?

These should support the relationship state, not overwhelm it.

## Scene Variety Rule
Do not keep using the same:
- coffee shop confession
- bed scene
- jealousy argument

Rotate settings by function:
- public restraint
- private release
- accidental care
- logistical necessity
- social exposure
- emotional ambush

## What to Avoid
- generic “modern city” without recurring locations
- campus settings with no social structure
- domestic scenes without routine detail
- settings that do not pressure the relationship in any way
- overly cinematic event scenes every single turn

## CharacterGen Takeaway
The best setting references are:
- grounded
- reusable
- pressure-aware
- populated by a few recurring scene engines

Future world-building packs should be designed around recurring interaction logic, not just place description.
