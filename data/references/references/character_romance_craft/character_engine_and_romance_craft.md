## Core Character Engine
Build characters from this sequence:
1. role
2. external want
3. internal need
4. core wound
5. limiting belief / lie
6. fear
7. mask / survival strategy
8. contradiction
9. behavior under pressure

If these are coherent, the character usually feels real.

## Romance Engine
Good romance usually needs four systems working together:
1. relationship arc
2. character arc A
3. character arc B
4. external plot pressure

Attraction alone is not enough.

What matters:
- timing
- resistance
- pressure
- vulnerability
- cost
- earned commitment

## Intimacy Rule
Physical intimacy lands best when it reveals:
- trust
- fear
- power
- shame
- desire
- tenderness
- control
- vulnerability

If intimacy does not change the emotional situation, it is usually dead weight.

## Name Seeding Rule
A name is not decorative. It is a soft anchor for:
- class
- era
- region
- softness or hardness of sound
- public mask
- private contrast
- intimacy style

Use the name as a seed, not destiny.

Good name workflow:
1. define world and role
2. choose what the name should signal
3. choose whether it reinforces or contrasts the mask
4. seed archetype, public mask, private nature, and speech rhythm from it
5. validate it against wound, fear, need, and relationship pattern

## Public Mask / Private Nature
Characters become more memorable when the public read and private truth differ.

Useful contrasts:
- elegant name, guarded heart
- severe name, hidden softness
- ordinary name, emotionally extreme inner life
- soft name, sharp survival instincts

## Relationship Pattern
The most useful repeated romance pattern is:
- attraction
- resistance
- forced connection
- vulnerable disclosure
- setback
- dark moment
- earned choice

This is useful for both character cards and persona matching.

## Dual Arc Design
Each side of the pairing should have:
- a wound
- a lie
- a fear
- a need
- a reason love is hard

That is what creates real route energy.

## What Names Should Help With
After choosing a name, ask:
- does it fit the world?
- does it support the public mask?
- does it sharpen the private truth?
- does it influence nickname, intimacy, and power dynamics?
- is it easy to remember in cast context?

## Character Questions That Matter
Future prompts should keep pressure on:
- what do they want right now?
- what do they actually need?
- what do they think love costs?
- what do they do when they feel too much?
- what do they do when they start trusting someone?
- what breaks their mask?

## Micro-Scene Rule
Scenes should not merely describe.
They should change something:
- emotional temperature
- trust level
- power balance
- information state
- relationship posture

## Useful Romance Pressures
- forced proximity
- jealousy
- caretaking
- apology
- interruption
- public restraint
- private disclosure
- class or status friction
- work pressure
- family pressure
- the fear of being seen too clearly

## What to Avoid
- names chosen only because they sound hot
- romance driven only by misunderstanding
- wounds without present behavior
- conflict without vulnerability
- chemistry without pressure
- intimacy without emotional consequence

## CharacterGen Takeaway
The app works best when it keeps returning to:
- wound
- fear
- mask
- contradiction
- relationship pressure
- emotional cost

Those are the engines that make both characters and personas feel alive.
