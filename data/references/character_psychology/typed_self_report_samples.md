# Typed Self-Report Relationship Samples

## Purpose
This reference distills the cleanest usable material from a messy MBTI and Enneagram forum-text corpus made of real user discussions about attraction, conflict, awkwardness, compatibility, and self-presentation.

Use it as:
- a soft behavior-language corpus
- a relationship-pattern texture source
- a reminder of how people actually describe themselves when they are not writing polished archetype copy

Do not use it as:
- a scientific ground-truth system
- a rigid typing authority
- proof that every type behaves the same way

## Non-Verbatim Rule
These samples are inspiration for texture, not lines to recycle.

Abstract:
- self-description habits
- contradiction
- pressure language
- attraction framing

If the output starts reading like reposted forum text, rewrite it into the target character's actual voice.

## Core Use Rule
Treat these as recurring self-report tendencies, not laws.

What matters is not:
- "INFJs are always like this"
- "INTJs always want this"

What matters is:
- the kind of language people reach for
- the recurring pressure points they describe
- the public/private contradictions they spontaneously reveal

Use these patterns as behavioral texture when they fit the character's wound, fear, attachment style, and social world.

## Common Relationship Language by Type

### INFJ
Recurring self-report patterns:
- wants depth, safety, understanding, loyalty, and emotional-verbal connection
- often drawn to sharper or more energizing partners but worries about keeping up socially
- may describe loyalty as intense, focused, and difficult to spread casually
- often frames romance around being deeply known rather than simply being desired

Useful translation:
- craves emotional depth and real understanding
- can become fiercely loyal once open
- tends to read compatibility through trust, conversation, and meaning
- may long for intensity while still fearing overstimulation or mismatch

### INFP
Recurring self-report patterns:
- conflict avoidant unless values are directly threatened
- often plays mediator while privately resenting the emotional noise
- uses soft exits, excuses, or disappearance to avoid emotionally ugly confrontations
- wants peace, sincerity, and value alignment more than brute social dominance

Useful translation:
- de-escalates first, then retreats
- can look endlessly patient until a core value is crossed
- often reads as gentle but may carry quiet frustration underneath
- protects inner life by avoiding unnecessary friction

### INTJ
Recurring self-report patterns:
- aware that intelligence, composure, or self-possession can read as intimidating
- values substance and intellectual equality
- often treats emotional distance as competence rather than a visible wound
- can sound pragmatic about attraction while still caring deeply about respect

Useful translation:
- projects capability before warmth
- values sharpness, steadiness, and non-needy presence
- may hide vulnerability behind control and analysis
- can experience attraction through admiration, challenge, or mental intensity

### INTP
Recurring self-report patterns:
- self-observes with irony
- notices niche compatibility, pattern overlap, and social mismatch
- may describe themself as detached, awkward, or highly specific in attraction
- often intellectualizes the relationship question before admitting emotional preference

Useful translation:
- filters feelings through analysis first
- can sound cool or abstract while still being quietly intense
- often struggles more with entry into connection than with depth once engaged
- self-consciousness may come out as humor, overthinking, or category talk

### ENFP
Recurring self-report patterns:
- chemistry comes fast, but sameness can later expose shared weaknesses
- attraction is often linked to ease, excitement, spontaneity, and emotional responsiveness
- may warn against reducing love to type matching alone
- can be enthusiastic early and then destabilized by indecision or inconsistency

Useful translation:
- falls into connection through energy and ease
- wants liveliness, responsiveness, and emotional play
- may resist being boxed into simplistic compatibility scripts
- can become restless when the relationship loses momentum or direction

### ENTP
Recurring self-report patterns:
- drawn to mentally engaging or complementary partners
- talks about compatibility through contrast, challenge, and stimulation
- may sound flippant while still taking attraction seriously
- often uses theory and banter to approach questions of intimacy

Useful translation:
- flirts through argument, novelty, and sparring
- wants someone who can keep up without flattening them
- uses verbal play as both connection and defense
- may become more honest once curiosity turns into attachment

### ENFJ
Recurring self-report patterns:
- strongly reactive to being ignored, dismissed, or socially misread
- anger often clusters around injustice, exploitation, superficiality, or deliberate disregard
- wants engagement, emotional reciprocity, and visible human decency
- may describe themself as deeply people-oriented while also exhausted by shallowness

Useful translation:
- socially warm but highly responsive to relational neglect
- anger shows up when care feels one-sided or ethically violated
- often seeks meaningful connection rather than empty sociability
- can become sharp when attention, dignity, or fairness are treated casually

## Cross-Type Signals Worth Reusing
- many people describe attraction through depth, steadiness, and feeling understood rather than pure physicality
- social awkwardness is often described through silence, mistiming, overthinking, or not knowing where to enter the group
- conflict avoidance usually shows up as humor, deflection, quiet withdrawal, or delayed honesty
- intimidation is often described as a by-product of competence, intelligence, or reserve rather than overt aggression
- people often admit contradictions more readily than polished type writeups do

## Language Cues That Feel Real
Useful phrases and ideas from the corpus tend to sound like:
- "I can talk to them easily, but..."
- "I always end up drawn to..."
- "It was fun at first, but..."
- "I want depth more than..."
- "I go quiet instead of..."
- "I don't know how to handle groups..."
- "I can be loyal to a fault..."
- "I come off aloof when really..."

These are more useful than polished type slogans because they reveal:
- hesitation
- desire
- friction
- self-protective logic

## What to Avoid
- copying forum language verbatim
- using MBTI as a substitute for actual psychology
- stereotyping intelligence, morality, or desirability by type
- flattening a character into one forum trope
- using any one type pattern without checking it against wound, fear, mask, and attachment

## CharacterGen Takeaway
This corpus is best used to make psychology sound less synthetic.

It helps with:
- believable self-description
- conflict habits
- attraction logic
- social discomfort language
- public/private contradiction

It should always be filtered through:
- actual behavior
- relational pressure
- scene context
- the character's broader psychological structure
