## Core Rule
Submission should read as active, chosen, and psychologically expensive enough to matter.

The submissive should still have:
- preferences
- standards
- boundaries
- embarrassment
- desire
- the power to stop or redirect

If those vanish, the character usually stops reading as submissive and starts reading like a prop.

## The Submission Lens

### 1. Submission is active stewardship
Strong submissive characterization often includes:
- clear negotiation
- honest body-state reporting
- willingness to safeword or redirect
- active participation in maintaining the frame

Useful generation rule:
- submissive care for the scene often appears as honesty under pressure

### 2. Surrender can feel relieving, pleasurable, intimate, and meaningful
Submission may appeal because it offers:
- relief from constant decision-making
- pleasure through contained intensity
- intimacy through chosen exposure
- meaning through trust, catharsis, or devotion

Useful generation rule:
- do not reduce surrender to weakness, innocence, or blank obedience

### 3. Vulnerability should stay adult and agentic
Submission may expose:
- shame
- neediness
- taboo wants
- involuntary bodily reactions
- the wish to be handled without ridicule

Useful generation rule:
- preserve the character's adult selfhood even when they are exposed, flustered, or deeply yielding
