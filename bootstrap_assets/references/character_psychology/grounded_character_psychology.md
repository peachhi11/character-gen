# Grounded Character Psychology

## Purpose
This reference folds in the strongest usable parts of:
- `Character Psychology & Human Behavior Guide.pdf`
- `How to craft characters.pdf`
- `1001 Character Quirks.pdf`
- `main_Advanced Personality Traits_world_info (1).json`

Use it to make characters and personas feel psychologically legible, behaviorally consistent, and more human under stress, intimacy, conflict, and attraction.

## Non-Verbatim Rule
This file should guide synthesis, not be echoed back as stored wording.

Use it as inspiration for:
- behavior logic
- emotional patterning
- contradiction
- pressure response

If output starts sounding like imported psychology notes instead of a live character, rewrite it into character-specific behavior.

## Core Rule
Psychology should always translate into visible behavior.

Do not stop at:
- labels
- diagnoses
- personality buzzwords
- trait lists without consequence

Always convert structure into:
- social style
- defensive style
- trust pattern
- conflict pattern
- attraction pattern
- body tells
- failure mode

## High-Value Psychological Layers

### Attachment
The cleanest relationship engine for roleplay is attachment style.

Use:
- secure
- anxious / preoccupied
- avoidant / dismissive
- disorganized / fearful

Translate into behavior:
- reassurance seeking
- distancing
- mixed signals
- cling-and-pullback patterns
- ease or difficulty with repair

### Defense Mechanisms
People protect themselves in patterned ways.

Useful examples:
- denial
- repression
- projection
- displacement
- rationalization
- reaction formation
- intellectualization

Use them to explain:
- why a character lies to themself
- why they misread others
- why they turn fear into sarcasm, competence, teasing, or anger

### Trauma Response
Stress often collapses into:
- fight
- flight
- freeze
- fawn

This is more useful in roleplay than abstract “traumatized” labeling.

### Values Hierarchy
Characters choose based on what they protect most:
- freedom
- love
- dignity
- safety
- family
- control
- status
- truth

Values explain hard choices better than adjectives do.

### Bias and Misinterpretation
Believable characters make believable errors.

Useful biases:
- confirmation bias
- catastrophizing
- mind reading
- black-and-white thinking
- self-serving interpretation

These help create:
- conflict
- jealousy
- insecurity
- bad calls that still make sense

## Trait Translation Rules
The advanced trait source is most useful when stripped down to:
- what the trait sounds like
- what it looks like
- how it affects touch, conflict, apology, flirting, and self-control

Example translation:
- affectionate:
  - speaks warmly
  - initiates touch
  - reassures often
  - seeks closeness when stressed
- arrogant:
  - dismisses weak framing
  - corrects others
  - resists vulnerability
  - hates public humiliation
- playful:
  - uses jokes and baiting
  - turns tension into games
  - flirts through movement and timing
  - hides seriousness until pushed
- groomed:
  - controlled presentation
  - precise language
  - order as self-regulation
  - discomfort with visible mess or sloppiness

## Quirks Rule
Quirks should come from:
- psychology
- stress
- upbringing
- desire
- control habits
- body-based self-soothing

Do not use quirks as random decoration.

Good quirks:
- linked to mood
- visible in scenes
- meaningful under pressure
- different in public vs private

Bad quirks:
- cute but irrelevant
- detached from behavior
- so loud they replace personality

## Public Mask vs Private Nature
Nearly every strong character needs a gap between:
- what others first read
- what is true underneath

Examples:
- composed outside, reassurance-hungry inside
- flirtatious outside, easily shamed inside
- cold outside, overprotective inside
- soft outside, steel-spined inside

This gap is one of the fastest ways to create depth.

## Pressure Behavior Matrix
Future prompts and validations should ask how the character changes under:
- shame
- rejection
- jealousy
- fear
- attraction
- conflict
- tenderness
- sexual vulnerability

That is where psychology becomes playable.

## Signs of Useful Psychological Depth
- reactions stay consistent with history
- contradictions feel human rather than random
- intimacy reveals deeper patterns instead of replacing them
- conflict exposes coping style
- speech shifts under pressure in recognizable ways

## What to Avoid
- therapy jargon without behavior
- stacking frameworks without translation
- turning every flaw into trauma spectacle
- quirks that read like random prompt filler
- flattening people into one dominant trait

## CharacterGen Takeaway
Psychology is most useful when it helps the app infer:
- what the character wants
- what they fear
- how they protect themselves
- how they misread love
- what their body gives away
- how they escalate or soften under pressure
