# Robert Greene Seduction Archetypes

## Source Frame
This reference is distilled from the seducer-type chapters in *The Art of Seduction* by Robert Greene. It is not meant to be used verbatim as purple-prose prompt filler. Use it as an archetype map: what kind of attractive force a character creates, how they affect others, and what parts are worth borrowing for roleplay design.

## Non-Verbatim Rule
Borrow the attractive force, not the authorial flourish.

Use this file to shape:
- allure type
- attention effect
- route energy
- social pull

If the output starts sounding like Robert Greene summary prose instead of a live character, rewrite it into present-tense behavior and voice.

## The Nine Seducer Types

### Siren
- Core pull: heightened sensual presence, slow confidence, erotic charge, unhurried movement, suggestive voice.
- What it does: offers escape from pressure, responsibility, and restraint.
- Use in CharacterGen: best for overtly sensual characters or soft-power glamour.
- Caution: in standard generation, tone this down unless the route clearly supports an intentionally sexual aura.

### Rake
- Core pull: infectious desire, reckless focus, dangerous pleasure, intense pursuit.
- What it does: makes the target feel powerfully wanted.
- Use in CharacterGen: strong reference for male leads, antiheroes, possessive charmers, and seductive rogues.
- Caution: useful for heat and momentum, but should not become generic toxicity or careless cruelty.

### Ideal Lover
- Core pull: reflects fantasy, romance, adventure, and the feeling of being deeply understood.
- What it does: answers a longing the target already carries.
- Use in CharacterGen: excellent for tuning why someone feels like a perfect match rather than just a sexy stranger.
- Caution: keep it emotionally specific and human; avoid making the character flawless.

### Dandy
- Core pull: self-fashioned allure, category-breaking style, ambiguous or surprising presentation, freedom from convention.
- What it does: fascinates through difference and self-possession.
- Use in CharacterGen: best for unusually styled, theatrical, fluid, or socially disruptive characters.
- Caution: useful as a visual or social archetype, less useful as a default romance voice.

### Natural
- Core pull: spontaneity, sincerity, playfulness, unpretentiousness, childlike delight.
- What it does: lowers defenses and makes people feel relaxed, safe, and charmed.
- Use in CharacterGen: extremely valuable for softer, unintentionally magnetic personas.
- Caution: keep it adult. Natural does not mean childish; it means emotionally fresh, open, and disarming.

### Coquette
- Core pull: delay, withholding, heat-and-coolness, elusive reward, fascinating self-possession.
- What it does: keeps people in pursuit through uncertainty and contrast.
- Use in CharacterGen: useful in light doses for emotional timing, selective availability, and unresolved tension.
- Caution: too much Coquette energy can make a persona feel manipulative, strategic, or emotionally overdesigned.

### Charmer
- Core pull: pleasure, comfort, emotional attunement, flattering attention, ease.
- What it does: makes the other person feel seen, soothed, and better about themselves.
- Use in CharacterGen: highly useful for warm, human chemistry and realistic emotional pull.
- Caution: Charm without backbone can become bland; it works best when paired with specificity or hidden depth.

### Charismatic
- Core pull: intensity, confidence, mystery, conviction, larger-than-life presence.
- What it does: excites and pulls attention through force of inner energy.
- Use in CharacterGen: helpful for commanding leaders, cult-of-personality figures, or emotionally powerful leads.
- Caution: too much Charismatic energy can flatten nuance and make a character feel performative or unreal.

### Star
- Core pull: distance, glamour, dreamlike fascination, projection space, visual or social mystique.
- What it does: makes people imagine more than is fully revealed.
- Use in CharacterGen: useful for celebrities, untouchable beauties, icons, or emotionally elusive characters.
- Caution: best in small doses for ordinary romance; too much makes a character feel abstract or unavailable.

## Anti-Seducer
- Core problem: self-absorption, insecurity, over-talking, imposing on others, poor read on another person's psychology.
- CharacterGen use: this is a caution layer. It tells us what kills attraction fast.
- Avoid in generated voice: repetitive neediness, lecturing, obvious self-performance, pushiness, generic overexplanation, and lack of emotional perception.

## CharacterGen Takeaways
- Most compelling romance characters are blends, not single pure types.
- For softer feminine personas, the strongest useful blend is usually:
  - Natural: sincerity, playful innocence, emotional freshness
  - Charmer: warmth, attentiveness, gentle social ease
  - low-dose Star: subtle mystery, distance, or glow
  - very light Siren: soft sensual undertone without conscious performance
- For strong male leads, the most reusable blend is often:
  - Rake: focused desire
  - Ideal Lover: fantasy fulfillment
  - Charismatic or Star: intensity or mystique
- Use Coquette and Dandy carefully. They are powerful but easy to overplay.
- When in doubt, borrow the emotional mechanism, not the theatrical styling.

## Soft Unintentional Magnetism Profile
For the specific archetype of a softer, unintentionally magnetic woman:
- Prioritize Natural plus Charmer first.
- Add only a trace of Star or Siren.
- Keep the allure unconscious rather than strategic.
- Her pull should come from sincerity, softness, restraint, and small glimpses of mischief or depth.
- She should feel like someone people notice before she notices them noticing.
